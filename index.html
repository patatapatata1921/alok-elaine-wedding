our story

chapter one: how we met

A girl from Malaysia. A boy from India. A fintech festival in Singapore that neither of us walked into looking for anything at all. We found each other somewhere between the panels and the coffee queues, and at the end of the night we shook hands. Nobody shakes hands. We both went home knowing something had already started.

chapter two: everywhere we went

It turned out we were both people who liked to leave. Weekends became trailheads and departure gates — helmets and headlamps underground, rice fields glowing outside a wooden window, temple spires on the far horizon. We got lost, got rained on, got hungry, and kept booking the next one. Somewhere in there, going anywhere together stopped being a plan and started being who we are.

chapter three: the next step

Then came the question neither of us had to think very hard about. A yes, a date, a stretch of coast in Kerala — and a future with enough room in it for everyone we love.

What to Wear

For the ladies
Sarees, lehengas or a smart dress

A silk or cotton saree, a lehenga or a salwar / kurta set. Rich, happy colours — maroon, mustard, emerald, pink, gold.

Prefer Western clothes? A bright, elegant midi or maxi dress with covered shoulders.

For the gentlemen
Kurta, mundu or a light suit

A kurta with trousers, or a sherwani / bandhgala for the evening. For a Kerala touch, the mundu — a white cotton wrap with a gold border, worn with a shirt.

Prefer Western clothes? A linen suit in a lighter colour.

Colours: avoid all-white and all-black, and leave bright red to the bride.

Cover up a little for the ceremony; a scarf or dupatta is handy.

Shoes may come off — choose slip-on sandals or flats. Fabric: cotton, linen and light silk for the warm weather.

Kerala Wedding 101

Kerala Wedding 101

Your first Indian wedding? Here's what to expect, so you can relax and enjoy every moment.

On the Day

Timing
Be a little early

The ceremony is set for an auspicious moment, the muhurtham, and it happens right on time — often quickly. Arrive 20–30 minutes early so you don't miss it.

Shoes
Shoes off for the rituals

You may be asked to remove your shoes near the ceremony area. Slip-on sandals make this easy.

Phones
Watch with your eyes

Our photographers will capture everything. During the key moments, please keep phones down and soak it in.

Customs & Etiquette

Greetings
Say "Namaskaram"

Palms together with a small nod is a warm, respectful hello to elders. Handshakes are fine with friends.

The feast
Eating a sadya

A Kerala feast is served on a banana leaf and eaten with the right hand — cutlery is always available if you'd prefer.

Gifts
Your presence is the gift

If you'd like to give something, a cash gift in an envelope is customary. Amounts ending in 1 (like 101 or 1,001) are considered lucky.

Mid-January in Kerala

Weather

Mid-January is one of the best times to visit — dry and sunny, around 23°C at night and 31–32°C during the day, and humid. Rain is rare at this time of year. Pack light, breathable clothes (cotton and linen), sunglasses, a hat and sunscreen; a light shawl is handy for air-conditioned rooms.

Stay well

Water & Health

Overseas guests: drink only sealed bottled water — and use it for brushing your teeth too. Check the seal is unbroken, skip ice in drinks outside the hotels, and go easy on raw salads and cut fruit from street stalls. Mosquito repellent and sunscreen are worth packing; hand sanitiser is handy.

Stay connected

Phone, eSIM & Internet

Having mobile data makes everything easier — Uber, maps, translating and staying in touch with us on WhatsApp.

Your options
  • Travel eSIM (easiest) — buy an India data eSIM online before you fly, install it at home, and switch it on when you land. Popular providers include Airalo → and Holafly →. First check your phone supports eSIM and is unlocked.
  • Roaming — check with your mobile provider at home; many offer India day passes. Turn off data roaming if you don't have a plan, to avoid surprise charges.
  • Local SIM card — Airtel and Jio have counters in the arrivals hall. Bring your passport and visa; activation can take a few hours, so it's best for longer stays.
  • Hotel Wi-Fi — all our recommended hotels have free Wi-Fi in rooms and lobbies.
Apps worth downloading before you fly
  • Uber — for every ride (sign in and add a payment card at home). App Store → Google Play →
  • Google Maps — download the Thiruvananthapuram area for offline use. App Store → Google Play →
  • Google Translate — download Malayalam for offline use; the camera can translate signs and menus. App Store → Google Play →
  • WhatsApp — the way everyone in India keeps in touch, including us. App Store → Google Play →
Charging
  • India uses 230V and plug types C, D and M (round pins). Bring a universal travel adapter.
⚠️ Caution — free public Wi-Fi
  • No banking on free Wi-Fi. At airports, cafés and malls, avoid online banking, payments or entering passwords. Use your mobile data instead.
  • Check the network name. Fake networks often copy real names (e.g. "Airport_Free_WiFi"). Ask staff for the exact name, and turn off "auto-join".
  • Never type an OTP or bank code into a pop-up or login page — no genuine Wi-Fi will ask for it.
  • Avoid public USB charging points. Use your own plug and cable, or a power bank.

Please read — staying safe

Keep yourself and your belongings safe

Kerala is friendly and welcoming, but like anywhere busy, visitors can be targeted. A few simple habits make all the difference:

  • Passport, cash and jewellery — keep them in your hotel safe. Carry a photo or photocopy of your passport instead.
  • Bags and phones — keep your bag zipped and in front of you, and your phone in a front pocket, especially in crowds, markets and temples.
  • Taxis — only use Uber or a car booked by your hotel. Politely say no to drivers or "helpers" who approach you at the airport or on the street.
  • Friendly strangers — be careful of anyone offering a tour, a shop, or saying a place is "closed today". A smile and "Venda" (no thanks) is enough.
  • Money — use ATMs inside banks or hotels, carry small notes, and count your change. Never share a bank code or OTP with anyone, even by phone.
  • Roads — traffic drives on the left and doesn't always stop. Cross slowly with others, and watch for uneven pavements and steps.
  • Heat — drink sealed bottled water, rest in the shade, and bring any medicines you need, with their prescriptions.
  • Stay together — go out in pairs, tell someone your plans, and keep your hotel's address and phone number with you.
  • Emergency numbers (free from any phone in India): 112All emergencies 100Police 108Ambulance 1363Tourist helpline (English)
